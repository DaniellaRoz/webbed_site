I Am Angry, And I Don't Know Why


I'd like to go back please.


I hate anger, I hate its irrational vortex that pulls you in.


It's like it has an event horizon. Once you are sufficiently angry it's like it's inevitable you will do something foolish. Something you will live to regret. Something the calm and lucid you would never begin to think of doing.


But it's cathartic.


There is such a magical catharsis in ignoring your better judgement and just, letting loose. Feeling every social expectation that pulls you down like a dress made of lead come off and doing what you so deepest desire.


We're denied this catharsis. I, you, every single soul on this planet are denied the deepest catharsis towards the absolutely rotten society that we have to exist within. We feel this directionless anger towards the world that we cannot channel, we cannot even comprehend the source of. We're angry at the world and we do not know why. Existing entirely ignorant to its root. Even the most naïve liberal sees that the churn of modern capitalist society is fundamentally broken, moulded through its entire core.


When was the last time you felt hope?


Genuine unambiguous hope, not just at some immediate future prospects, but the future prospects of your life as a whole, the future prospect of society.


It's been a while, hasn't it? Probably not since you were a child, a really young child, before you had social media, perhaps before you even started school.


When I was a teenager I was a Marxist-Leninist. The Marxist-Leninist cliques, at least the strand that persisted on Reddit at places like r/MoreTankieChapo and r/GenZedong1, were filled with this hopeless optimism that persisted through Cuba and especially China. «Communism Will Win» already a childish slogan, started being replaced with just «China Will Win». American and European teenagers with the token disgruntled Hongkonger gathering around online to put all their hope to be pulled out of their sad hopeless life into the PRC. Into Xi Jinping personally.


This tangent was certainly built on a decent bit of past grudges, but I present this because it's a key part in the magical thinking in modern pseudo-religions that have sprung up. That it's Marxist-Leninist is arbitrary, replace this with any political thought you want. Politics has removed itself from its primary purpose as actually affecting change and controlling governance and into a game of loyalty rituals and folk religion.


This is far from an original observation, but it's one that I personally came to realise quite recently. And is a key part in why I now outright reject any political labels. This is not me somehow thinking I'm «better» than all these political-theists. I think I'm just a bit more honest and open about engaging in magical thinking through Buddhism, rather than through a political cult.


There is a reason I don't specify online. IRL political organisation is often mythologised, especially by these online political circles. One has to go «organise» for posting on Reddit is a waste of time2 and won't bring the revolution. But having actually partaken in political organisation in a party (Rødt), I am left with even less hope. In the last national poll, Rødt placed 4th largest in Norway at around 7% of the share. And yet in Rødt I do not see a team of professional and respectible people trying to better the world. It's pathetically childish people whose political analysis is fully based on vibes and what they already believed before starting to investigate an issue.


They don't know what they're doing. Liberalism is sophistry all the way down.


I used to joke that 東方星蓮船touhou seirensen　〜 Undefined Fantastic Object made me a Buddhist, but if I were to seriously reflect over why I became a Buddhist I think the answer would be joining Rødt. Through Buddhism I haven't magically found peace with the world, but I've stopped clutching this naïve liberal hope in the ability to better the world and win over my so called enemies.


But despite the peace it gives me, I do not get the catharsis I still so desperately seek. I still feel the simmering anger at the world boiling up. Whenever I see something morally repugnant I retreat into Buddhist faith, and while it calms me, I cannot get the catharsis so cruelly denied.


So I sit here, angry at the world, so desperate to let my anger out. Yet I don't even know what I am angry at. I can list a million things about the world that trouble me. But when I read the news I don't get angry at it. I just feel despair and demotivation. It's like the anger does not come from the atomic events themselves, but rather the greater complex of suffering this world has become. This directionless juvenile anger as I kick and scream against the terrifying machine of capitalism, which stares intently at me with its salivating maw.


I am angry, and I don't know why.


All I know is that I want someone to suffer for it.
